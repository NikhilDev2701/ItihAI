# Ellora Caves

## Overview
The Ellora Caves are a monumental complex of 34 rock-cut monasteries and temples excavated side-by-side along a 2-kilometer basalt escarpment in the Charanandri hills of Aurangabad district, Maharashtra, India. Dating from the 6th to 10th centuries CE, Ellora is renowned for its uninterrupted co-existence of Buddhist, Hindu, and Jain sanctuaries and contains the monolithic Kailasa Temple. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

## Historical Background
Excavation took place between 600 and 1000 CE under the patronage of the Rashtrakuta and Yadava dynasties:
- **Buddhist Caves (Caves 1–12)**: Carved between 600–730 CE, featuring the famous multi-storey Vishwakarma "Carpenter's" Cave.
- **Hindu Caves (Caves 13–29)**: Carved between 600–850 CE, culminating in the monolithic Kailasa Temple (Cave 16) commissioned by Rashtrakuta King Krishna I (r. 756–773 CE).
- **Jain Caves (Caves 30–34)**: Excavated in the 9th and 10th centuries under Digambara Jain patrons, including the exquisite Indra Sabha (Cave 32).

## Cultural Significance
Ellora is an extraordinary testament to religious pluralism, tolerance, and harmonious co-existence in ancient India. It proves that major religious traditions lived, worshipped, and created side-by-side without sectarian conflict.

## Architecture
- **Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)**: The largest monolithic rock-cut structure in the world. Carved top-down from a single basalt cliff face:
  - Estimated 200,000 to 400,000 tons of solid rock were excavated over several decades.
  - The temple stands 32 meters high, 85 meters long, and 47 meters wide (twice the footprint area of the Parthenon in Athens).
  - Features life-sized monolithic elephants, a 30-meter high entrance gopuram, two 15-meter victory pillars (*Dhvajastambhas*), and massive multi-level Dravidian sanctum towers.
- **Vishvakarma Cave (Cave 10)**: A cathedral-like Buddhist Chaitya hall with ribbed stone ceilings imitating wooden carpentry and a 15-foot seated Buddha image.
- **Indra Sabha (Cave 32)**: A two-tiered Jain sanctuary renowned for delicate lotus ceilings, floral pillars, and statues of Bahubali and Parsvanatha.

## Important Facts
- The Kailasa Temple was excavated vertically from above down to the courtyard floor, meaning sculptors had no margin for error or structural additions.
- Traces of original white lime plaster remain on Kailasa's exterior, which was once coated to make it appear like the snow-clad Mount Kailash.

## Local Traditions
- **Ellora-Ajanta Festival**: Annual winter cultural festival featuring Indian classical musicians and dancers against illuminated cave facades.
- **Grishneshwar Pilgrimage**: Devotees often combine a visit to Ellora with prayers at the nearby Grishneshwar Jyotirlinga.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Grishneshwar Temple** (1 km): One of the twelve sacred Jyotirlinga shrines dedicated to Lord Shiva.
- **Daulatabad Fort** (15 km): Formidable medieval fortress with a subterranean dark labyrinth (*Andhari*).
- **Khuldabad** (3 km): Historic walled town housing the simple tomb of Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: Sunrise to Sunset daily (Closed on Tuesdays).
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹600.
- **Dress Code**: Modest attire; sturdy walking shoes for stairs and rocky terrain.
- **Photography**: Permitted throughout the complex.
