# Fatehpur Sikri

## Overview
Fatehpur Sikri ("City of Victory") is a fortified palace complex and city located in Agra district, Uttar Pradesh, India. Built entirely from local red sandstone, it served as the capital of the Mughal Empire under Emperor Akbar from 1571 to 1585. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

## Historical Background
Emperor Akbar had no surviving heirs and sought the blessings of the Sufi mystic Sheikh Salim Chishti in the village of Sikri. Following the saint's prophecy, Akbar's wife gave birth to a son, Prince Salim (later Emperor Jahangir) in 1569. In gratitude, Akbar founded a new capital city here in 1571. After his successful military conquest of Gujarat in 1573, the city was named Fatehpur Sikri. The city was peacefully abandoned in 1585 due to an insufficient water supply and Akbar's strategic campaigns in the northwest.

## Cultural Significance
Fatehpur Sikri is the architectural embodiment of Akbar's progressive cultural and religious philosophy (*Sulh-i-Kul* — universal peace). It was here in the Ibadat Khana that Akbar convened theological debates among scholars of Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Zoroastrianism, and Jainism, formulating the syncretic ethical code *Din-i Ilahi*.

## Architecture
- **Buland Darwaza ("Gate of Magnificence")**: Standing 54 meters (177 ft) high from the ground, the highest gateway in the world, erected in 1601 to commemorate the conquest of Gujarat.
- **Tomb of Sheikh Salim Chishti**: An exquisite white marble mausoleum enclosed in delicate pierced marble lattice screens (*jalis*), located inside the red sandstone Jama Masjid courtyard.
- **Diwan-i-Khas (Hall of Private Audience)**: Renowned for its central single stone pillar carved with 36 serpentine brackets in the form of a lotus capital supporting a circular platform connected to four elevated walkways.
- **Panch Mahal**: A five-storey stepped pyramidal pavilion with 176 unique sculpted columns designed for court recreation.
- **Anup Talao**: A central ornamental pool with a floating central platform where legendary court musician Tansen performed musical ragas.

## Important Facts
- The famous Persian inscription on the Buland Darwaza reads: *"Jesus, son of Mary, said: The world is a bridge, pass over it, but build no houses upon it. He who hopes for an hour may hope for eternity."*
- Unlike medieval fortified capitals built over generations, Fatehpur Sikri was conceived and completely constructed within a single 15-year period.

## Local Traditions
- **Tying Wish Threads (*Dhaga*)**: Visitors and pilgrims of all faiths tie red and yellow sacred threads to the marble jali screens of Salim Chishti's tomb while making a wish, returning to untie a thread when the wish comes true.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Taj Mahal & Agra Fort** (36 km): Agra's primary Mughal landmarks.
- **Keoladeo National Park / Bharatpur Bird Sanctuary** (25 km): UNESCO World Heritage wetland wildlife sanctuary.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: Sunrise to Sunset daily.
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹610.
- **Dress Code**: Modest attire; head covering required inside the Dargah courtyard; remove shoes before entering the mosque grounds.
- **Photography**: Permitted across all public courtyards.
