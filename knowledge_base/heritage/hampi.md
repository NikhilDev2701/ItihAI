# Group of Monuments at Hampi

## Overview
The Group of Monuments at Hampi comprises the ruined monumental capital of the Vijayanagara Empire, located along the Tungabhadra River in Vijayanagara district, Karnataka, India. Spread across more than 4,100 hectares (16 sq mi) of surreal boulder-strewn terrain, Hampi was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.

## Historical Background
Founded in 1336 by the warrior brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I of the Sangama dynasty, Hampi (ancient *Vijayanagara*, the "City of Victory") rose to become the richest and most powerful empire in South India. During its zenith under Emperor Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509–1529), travelers from Portugal, Persia, and Venice (such as Domingo Paes and Abdur Razzaq) described Hampi as a metropolis rivaling Rome and Beijing in size, where diamonds and emeralds were traded by the measure in open-air bazaars. In 1565, after the Battle of Talikota, the city was conquered, sacked, and abandoned to the elements.

## Cultural Significance
Hampi is identified in Indian tradition with *Kishkindha*, the monkey kingdom described in the ancient epic *Ramayana*. The Virupaksha Temple has maintained an unbroken tradition of active Hindu worship since at least the 7th century CE. Hampi represents the peak of Vijayanagara architecture and South Indian artistic patronage.

## Architecture
- **Dravidian Vijayanagara Style**: Massive granite construction, towering stepped gopurams, pillared *mandapas* with rearing mythical yalis, and integrated water systems.
- **Key Monuments**:
  - *Vittala Temple Complex*: The crown jewel of Hampi featuring the iconic **Stone Chariot** (a monolithic shrine dedicated to Garuda with rotating stone wheels) and the celebrated hall of **56 Musical Pillars** (*SaReGaMa pillars*) that produce acoustic resonant musical tones when gently struck.
  - *Virupaksha Temple*: 50-meter-high eastern gopuram overlooking the 700-meter-long Hampi Bazaar street.
  - *Royal Enclosure*: The Mahanavami Dibba stepped ceremonial platform, underground secret council chambers, and stepped geometric public baths (*Pushkarani*).
  - *Zenana Enclosure*: Indo-Islamic influenced Lotus Mahal and the grand 11-domed Elephant Stables.

## Important Facts
- Hampi was the second-largest city in the medieval world after Beijing in the early 16th century.
- The musical pillars were carved from single solid granite monoliths without internal hollow chambers, relying entirely on stone density variations for resonance.
- The Stone Chariot is featured on the reverse of the modern Indian ₹50 banknote.

## Local Traditions
- **Coracle Crossing**: Navigating the sacred Tungabhadra in round wicker-and-canvas boats (*coracles*) used since ancient times.
- **Hampi Utsav**: A grand three-day cultural festival held every winter celebrating the music, dance, and heritage of Karnataka.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Matanga Hill** (1.5 km): Highest elevation in central Hampi offering panoramic 360-degree sunrise and sunset views.
- **Anegundi** (across the river): Ancient fortified village considered the historic core of Kishkindha.
- **Daroji Sloth Bear Sanctuary** (18 km): Wildlife sanctuary protecting native sloth bears.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: November to February (avoid hot summer months).
- **Hours**: Sunrise to Sunset daily.
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹600 (Composite pass for Vittala Temple, Lotus Mahal, and ASI museum).
- **Dress Code**: Modest clothing required inside functioning temples (cover shoulders and knees); remove shoes at sanctums.
- **Photography**: Permitted throughout ruins; restricted inside sanctums.
