# Konark Sun Temple

## Overview
The Konark Sun Temple is a monumental 13th-century CE Hindu temple dedicated to the Sun God Surya, situated at Konark on the coastline of Puri district in Odisha, India. Designed as a colossal stone chariot with 24 carved wheels drawn by seven horses, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

## Historical Background
Commissioned around 1250 CE by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga Dynasty to commemorate military victories against regional invaders. The temple was built near the mouth of the Chandrabhaga River along ancient maritime trade routes. European mariners referred to it as the "Black Pagoda" because of its dark granite stone profile visible from the Bay of Bengal. Over centuries, environmental erosion and soil subsidence caused the soaring main sanctuary tower (Vimana) to collapse, but the surviving assembly hall (Jagamohana) and dance pavilion (Natamandira) remain architectural marvels.

## Cultural Significance
Konark represents the climax of Kalinga temple architecture. In Hindu solar theology, Surya crosses the sky daily in a cosmic chariot. The temple visually manifests this cosmological order. Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore famously stated: *"Here the language of stone surpasses the language of human."*

## Architecture
- **Chariot Conception**: The entire temple platform forms Surya's cosmic chariot with 24 elaborately carved stone wheels (nearly 3 meters in diameter each) pulled by seven galloping horses.
- **Astronomical Precision**: Each wheel functions as an accurate sundial. The shadows cast by the 8 major and 8 minor spokes provide time measurements down to minutes.
- **Kalinga Style**: Built with Khondalite and Chlorite stone, characterized by Pidha Deula assembly hall and intricate friezes depicting musicians, celestial dancers (*Alasa Kanyas*), royal processions, and scenes of daily life.

## Important Facts
- The 24 wheels symbolize the 24 hours of a day (or 12 pairs of lunar fortnights), while the 7 horses represent the days of the week or the seven colors of sunlight.
- The Natamandira (Dance Hall) contains over 128 carved postures of classical Odissi dance recorded in stone.
- The wheels appear on the reverse of the modern Indian ₹10 currency note.

## Local Traditions
- **Konark Dance Festival**: Held annually from December 1 to 5 against the illuminated temple backdrop, gathering world-renowned classical Indian dancers.
- **Magha Saptami Festival**: Pilgrims gather at dawn on Chandrabhaga Beach to offer prayers to the rising Sun.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Chandrabhaga Beach** (3 km): Pristine shoreline famous for sunrises and blue-flag ecotourism.
- **Puri Jagannath Temple** (35 km): One of the four holiest Char Dham pilgrimage shrines in India.
- **Raghurajpur Heritage Crafts Village** (40 km): Traditional village renowned for Pattachitra scroll painting.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: November to February.
- **Hours**: 6:00 AM to 8:00 PM daily.
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹600.
- **Dress Code**: Light cotton attire; remove footwear before ascending stone plinths.
- **Photography**: Permitted throughout the grounds.
