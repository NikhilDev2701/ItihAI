# Mahabodhi Temple Complex

## Overview
The Mahabodhi Temple Complex (literally "Great Awakening Temple") is an ancient Buddhist temple complex located in Bodh Gaya, Gaya district, Bihar, India. It marks the exact location where Prince Siddhartha Gautama attained supreme enlightenment (Bodhi) around 531 BCE and became the Buddha. Designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2002, it is the most sacred pilgrimage destination for Buddhists worldwide.

## Historical Background
Around 250 BCE, Emperor Ashoka visited Bodh Gaya to establish a shrine and monastery next to the sacred Bodhi Tree, creating the Diamond Throne (*Vajrasana*) to mark the exact spot of enlightenment. The present monumental grand pyramidal brick temple structure was constructed during the Late Gupta period (5th–6th centuries CE) and represents one of the earliest brick temples surviving in India. After centuries of neglect following the 13th century, the complex was comprehensively restored in the late 19th century under Sir Alexander Cunningham and the Archaeological Survey of India.

## Cultural Significance
The Mahabodhi Temple is the spiritual epicentre of global Buddhism. It encompasses the Seven Sacred Sites associated with the seven weeks the Buddha spent in meditation following enlightenment, including the Bodhi Tree, the Animeshlocha Stupa, the Ratnachakrama (jeweled cloister walk), and the Muchalinda Lake.

## Architecture
- **Central Shikhara (Tower)**: A massive 55-meter-high (180 ft) stepped pyramidal tower rising from a square base, capped by a gilded stupa finial.
- **Corner Towers**: Four smaller subsidiary corner towers echoing the central spire's architecture.
- **Ashokan Railings**: 1st-century BCE carved sandstone and granite railings (*Vedika*) encircling the inner sanctum, carved with medallions of lotus flowers, mythical beasts (*Makaras*), and celestial scenes.
- **The Bodhi Tree & Vajrasana**: Located directly behind the western wall of the temple. The Vajrasana is a polished red sandstone slab placed by Ashoka to denote the "navel of the earth".

## Important Facts
- The sacred Bodhi Tree (*Ficus religiosa*) standing today is revered as a direct botanical descendant of the original tree under which the Buddha sat in meditation.
- In 2013, the sanctum tower dome was gilded with over 290 kilograms of pure gold donated by the King of Thailand and Buddhist devotees.
- Over 50 international Buddhist monasteries and temples representing Sri Lanka, Thailand, Myanmar, Japan, Tibet, Bhutan, Vietnam, and Cambodia are active in Bodh Gaya.

## Local Traditions
- **Meditation & Chanting**: Pilgrims and monks gather in silent seated meditation beneath the Bodhi tree from dawn until nightfall.
- **Butter Lamp Offerings**: Lighting thousands of small clay butter lamps around the temple stupa courtyard at sunset.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Great Buddha Statue** (1.5 km): Monumental 80-foot (24 m) stone statue of Buddha seated in lotus position.
- **Dungeshwari (Mahakala) Caves** (12 km): Rock caves where Siddhartha practiced severe austerities for six years prior to reaching Bodh Gaya.
- **International Monasteries Route** (around Bodh Gaya): Architectural tour of authentic pagodas from across Asia.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: 5:00 AM to 9:00 PM daily.
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: Free entry (small camera fee applies).
- **Dress Code**: Modest and respectful attire covering shoulders and knees; footwear must be deposited at the free counter outside.
- **Photography**: Allowed with an official camera ticket; mobile phones must be placed in lockers at the security checkpoint.
