# Qutub Minar

## Overview
Qutub Minar is a soaring 72.5-meter (238 ft) tall fluted minaret situated in the Mehrauli area of Southwest Delhi, India. It is the tallest brick minaret in the world and forms the centerpiece of the Qutb Complex, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993.

## Historical Background
Construction was initiated in 1192 by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, founder of the Delhi Sultanate, following the victory over Delhi's Chauhan rulers. Aibak completed only the basement level. His successor and son-in-law, Shams-ud-din Iltutmish, added three storeys around 1220. After a lightning strike damaged the top storey in 1368, Sultan Firoz Shah Tughlaq repaired and added two replacement storeys using red sandstone and white marble.

## Cultural Significance
Qutub Minar marks the beginning of Islamic rule in North India and the birth of Indo-Islamic architecture, where local Indian stonemasons incorporated indigenous decorative motifs (such as lotus garlands and bell ropes) alongside Arabic calligraphic bands and geometric arches.

## Architecture
- **Dimensions**: 72.5 meters high with a base diameter of 14.3 meters tapering to 2.7 meters at the top.
- **Storeys**: Five distinct storeys with cantilevered balconies supported by intricate stalactite brackets (corbels).
- **Materials**: The lower three storeys are constructed of fluted red and buff sandstone; the top two storeys combine white marble and red sandstone.
- **Inscriptions**: Elaborate bands of Quranic calligraphy in Naskh and Kufic scripts alternated with geometric bands.

## Important Facts
- **The Rustless Iron Pillar**: Standing in the adjoining mosque courtyard, this 6-ton, 7.2-meter iron column dates to the 4th century CE (Gupta period) and has remained completely corrosion-free for over 1,600 years due to high phosphorus content and passive oxide films.
- **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque**: The oldest surviving mosque built by the Delhi Sultans.
- **Alai Minar**: An unfinished rival minaret started by Alauddin Khilji in 1311 intended to stand twice as tall as Qutub Minar.

## Local Traditions
- **Iron Pillar Wishing Legend**: Historical folklore held that circling the pillar with your back against it and joining hands brought fulfillment of wishes (the pillar is now protected by a railing).
- **Sufi Heritage Walks**: Mehrauli is one of Delhi's oldest continuously inhabited cultural quarters with historic spiritual shrines.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Mehrauli Archaeological Park** (800 m): Sprawling historical reserve with over 100 Sultanate and Mughal ruins.
- **Garden of Five Senses** (2.5 km): Contemporary landscaped park featuring public art and cultural exhibits.
- **Hauz Khas Complex** (5 km): Medieval water reservoir and madrasa complex.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: Sunrise to Sunset daily (Open 7 days).
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹550.
- **Dress Code**: Comfortable walking clothes; sunscreen recommended.
- **Photography**: Permitted throughout the grounds.
