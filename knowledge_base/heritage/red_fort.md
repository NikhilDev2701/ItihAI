# Red Fort (Lal Qila)

## Overview
The Red Fort (Lal Qila) is a historic fortified palace complex located in Old Delhi, India. Constructed from red sandstone along the Yamuna River, it served as the principal imperial residence and administrative center of the Mughal Empire for almost two centuries. It was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007.

## Historical Background
In 1638, Emperor Shah Jahan decided to move his capital from Agra to a newly planned city in Delhi named Shahjahanabad. Construction of the fort began under architect Ustad Ahmad Lahori in May 1638 and concluded in April 1648. It remained the seat of imperial authority until the 1857 Indian Uprising, after which British colonial forces occupied the fort. On August 15, 1947, Jawaharlal Nehru hoisted the flag of independent India from the ramparts of Lahori Gate, establishing an unbroken national tradition.

## Cultural Significance
The Red Fort is an iconic symbol of Indian sovereignty and modern nationhood. It represents the highest achievement of Mughal urban planning, blending courtly ceremonial dignity with sophisticated gardens, residential pavilions, and markets.

## Architecture
The fortress is enclosed within massive octagonal red sandstone walls stretching 2.4 kilometers:
- **Lahori Gate**: The ceremonial main entrance facing the historic market street of Chandni Chowk.
- **Chatta Chowk**: A covered vaulted bazaar where court artisans sold luxury goods.
- **Diwan-i-Aam**: The Hall of Public Audience, featuring an ornate marble throne canopy (*Jharokha*).
- **Diwan-i-Khas**: The Hall of Private Audience, adorned with white marble, floral inlays, and the celebrated Persian inscription: *"Agar firdaus bar ru-yi zamin ast, hamin ast u hamin ast u hamin ast"* (If there is a paradise on earth, it is this, it is this, it is this).
- **Nahr-i-Bihisht**: The "Canal of Paradise" channels that brought cool water through the imperial pavilions.

## Important Facts
- The fort was originally partially white (lime plaster) and red; the British painted damaged sections red in the late 19th century.
- The famed Peacock Throne (*Takht-i-Taus*) sat in the Diwan-i-Khas before it was carried away by Persian ruler Nadir Shah in 1739.
- Every year on Independence Day (August 15), the Prime Minister delivers the national address from the fort ramparts.

## Local Traditions
- **Independence Day Flag Hoisting**: A sacred national civic occasion broadcast across the country.
- **Evening Sound and Light Show**: Dramatic historic narration recounting 500 years of Delhi history in Hindi and English.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Jama Masjid** (1 km): One of India's largest congregational mosques built by Shah Jahan in 1656.
- **Chandni Chowk** (500 m): Bustling medieval market famous for street food, textiles, and spices.
- **Raj Ghat** (2 km): Memorial to Mahatma Gandhi on the banks of the Yamuna.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: 9:30 AM to 4:30 PM (Closed on Mondays).
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹550.
- **Dress Code**: Comfortable walking clothes and walking shoes.
- **Photography**: Allowed throughout outdoor courtyards.
