# Sanchi Stupa

## Overview
The Great Stupa at Sanchi is an ancient Buddhist monument located on a hilltop in Sanchi, Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh, India. Dating from the 3rd century BCE, it is the oldest intact stone structure in India and was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1989.

## Historical Background
Originally commissioned in the 3rd century BCE by Mauryan Emperor Ashoka the Great, whose wife Devi was a native of nearby Vidisha. Ashoka built a hemispherical brick stupa to enshrine corporal relics of the Buddha. During the Shunga period (2nd century BCE), the stupa was enlarged with stone casing to twice its original size. Under the Satavahana dynasty in the 1st century BCE, four magnificent carved stone gateways (Toranas) and an elevated balustrade were added. Sanchi remained an active Buddhist monastery complex until the 12th century CE before being rediscovered in 1818 by British General Henry Taylor.

## Cultural Significance
Sanchi is a foundational monument of Buddhist art and architecture. It illustrates the transition from aniconic representation of the Buddha (represented through symbols like the Dharma wheel, footprints, umbrella, and lotus) to narrative storytelling, depicting the propagation of peace, compassion, and Ashoka's *Dhamma*.

## Architecture
- **Anda (Hemispherical Dome)**: Solid sandstone hemispherical dome measuring 36.5 meters in diameter and 16.4 meters in height, symbolizing the cosmic vault of heaven.
- **Harmika**: A square balcony-like railing atop the dome representing the sacred dwelling of the gods.
- **Chattra**: A three-tiered stone umbrella representing the Three Jewels of Buddhism (Buddha, Dharma, Sangha).
- **Toranas (Four Gateways)**: Standing facing the four cardinal directions, each consisting of two square pillars supporting three architraves carved with exquisite relief sculptures of Jataka tales, historical events, and guardian spirits (*Yakshis*).
- **Pradakshina Path**: An elevated circumambulatory walkway enclosed by stone railings (*Vedika*).

## Important Facts
- The famous Ashoka Lion Capital was also erected at Sanchi; while damaged, it is preserved in the on-site archaeological museum.
- The southern gateway is the oldest of the four toranas, containing inscriptions mentioning donations by the ivory carvers of Vidisha.
- The site was meticulously restored between 1912 and 1919 under Sir John Marshall, Director-General of the ASI.

## Local Traditions
- **Circumambulation (Pradakshina)**: Pilgrims and visitors walk clockwise around the stupa base and upper terrace in mindful contemplation.
- **Chetiyagiri Vihara Festival**: Annual international gathering of Buddhist monks and pilgrims in late November.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Udayagiri Caves** (12 km): 5th-century Gupta-era rock-cut sanctuary caves featuring the monumental Varaha panel.
- **Vidisha Archaeological Museum** (9 km): Houses the famous Besnagar Yakshi and ancient sculptures.
- **Heliodorus Pillar** (11 km): 2nd-century BCE stone column erected by Greek ambassador Heliodorus.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March.
- **Hours**: Sunrise to Sunset daily.
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹600 (Includes entry to on-site Archaeological Museum).
- **Dress Code**: Modest attire; respectful behavior in monument grounds.
- **Photography**: Permitted around the monuments.
