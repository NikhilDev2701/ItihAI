# Taj Mahal

## Overview
The Taj Mahal is an ivory-white marble mausoleum on the south bank of the Yamuna River in Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India. Universally admired as the pinnacle of Mughal architecture and one of the Seven Wonders of the Modern World, it was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983.

## Historical Background
Commissioned in 1632 by the fifth Mughal Emperor Shah Jahan to enshrine the tomb of his favorite wife, Arjumand Banu Begum (Mumtaz Mahal), who died during childbirth in Burhanpur in 1631. The primary mausoleum was completed in 1648, while surrounding pavilions, the Great Gate (Darwaza-i-Rauza), mosque, guest house (Jawab), and Charbagh gardens were finished by 1653. The construction employed over 20,000 artisans under the supervision of a royal board of architects led by Ustad Ahmad Lahori.

## Cultural Significance
The Taj Mahal represents the zenith of the syncretic Indo-Islamic artistic tradition. In Persian and Mughal philosophical symbolism, the monument and its quadripartite gardens are constructed as an earthly manifestation of paradise (Jannat). It stands as a world-renowned cultural ambassador of Indian artistic craftsmanship.

## Architecture
The complex follows a strict bilaterally symmetrical grid:
- **Central Dome**: A double-shelled onion dome rising 73 meters (240 ft) above ground, crowned with a gilded lotus finial.
- **Pietra Dura (Parchin Kari)**: Highly refined decorative stone inlays using lapis lazuli, jasper, turquoise, jade, and carnelian set into white Makrana marble.
- **Minarets**: Four three-storey minarets standing 40 meters tall at the corners of the plinth, deliberately canted slightly outward.
- **Charbagh Gardens**: Persian quadripartite paradise garden divided by intersecting water channels reflecting the central mausoleum.

## Important Facts
- The marble shifts tones during the day: blush pink at sunrise, milk-white at noon, and golden bronze under moonlight.
- The only asymmetrical feature in the complex is the tomb of Shah Jahan, which was installed beside Mumtaz Mahal's central tomb following his death in 1666.
- The four minarets tilt outward by approximately 2 degrees to protect the central crypt in case of an earthquake.

## Local Traditions
- **Footwear Protocol**: Visitors must remove footwear or use protective shoe covers on the marble plinth to prevent abrasion of the Makrana stone.
- **Friday Congregational Prayers**: The monument is closed to tourist entry every Friday for afternoon prayers at the mosque inside the complex.

## Nearby Attractions
- **Agra Fort** (2.5 km): Massive red sandstone fortress and seat of Mughal power until 1638.
- **Fatehpur Sikri** (36 km): Preserved 16th-century Mughal capital city.
- **Mehtab Bagh** (3.2 km): The Moonlight Garden across the river providing unobstructed sunset views.

## Visitor Information
- **Best Time to Visit**: October to March (pleasant temperatures).
- **Hours**: 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes before sunset (Closed on Fridays).
- **Foreign Tourist Entry**: ₹1,100 + ₹200 for main mausoleum access.
- **Dress Code**: Modest attire covering shoulders and knees.
- **Photography**: Permitted in the gardens and courtyards; strictly prohibited in the interior cenotaph chamber.
